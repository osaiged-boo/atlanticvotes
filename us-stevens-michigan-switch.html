Stevens defends her Michigan switch: truth over political convenience

November 4, 2026

Representative Haley Stevens has explained her last-minute break with her own party’s Senate nominee in Michigan, saying she put truth over political convenience. In an article posted on X on November 4, titled “Truth Over Political Convenience,” the Michigan Democrat said she did not regret the decision itself, while apologising for the upset it caused colleagues and people she cares about.

The candidate at the centre of it is Abdul El-Sayed, the Democrat who defeated Stevens in Michigan’s August Senate primary. Stevens endorsed El-Sayed after that primary, then withdrew her support two days before the general election and endorsed Republican Mike Rogers instead. Reporting on the switch put two grievances at its heart: El-Sayed’s campaign appearances with political streamer Hasan Piker, and an outside PAC advertisement linking United States aid to Israel with rising grocery prices, which Stevens called antisemitic. Stevens also said El-Sayed’s campaign had told her she was no longer welcome in it.

Stevens pointed in her November 4 article to a strongly pro-Israel voting record built over eight years in Congress, and to her role as co-chair of the House caucus on antisemitism. She said she has stood with the Jewish community in easier and harder times and will continue to do so, and that she had hoped the winner of the election could be moderated and supported after it. In her account, that hope did not hold.

The switch did not change the outcome. El-Sayed won the open seat on November 3, defeating Rogers by 2,505,618 votes to 2,170,524, with about 97 per cent of the expected vote counted, according to the Associated Press figures used in our Michigan Senate review. He will become the first Muslim American elected to the United States Senate.

Stevens closed by saying she was elected to do a job and will finish it, that she remains a Democrat, and by congratulating Tuesday’s winners. Read her article on X.
