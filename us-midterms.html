The stakes

What control looks like going in

These are the starting numbers, not results. A majority in the House is 218 seats; in the Senate it is 51 seats, or 50 plus the Vice President's tie-breaking vote.

218–214House going in: 218 Republican, 214 Democrat, 1 independent, 2 vacant. Democrats need a net gain of 4 seats to reach 218.
53–47Senate going in: 53 Republican, 47 Democrat (including 2 independents caucusing with Democrats). Democrats need a net gain of 4 for 51; a 50–50 tie stays Republican via the tiebreaker.
26–24Governorships going in: 26 Republican, 24 Democrat. Of the 36 governorships on the ballot, 18 are held by each party.
40Key races tracked here: 13 Senate, 17 House, 10 governor races rated competitive by the Cook Political Report.

Of the 35 Senate seats on the ballot, 22 are Republican-held and 13 Democratic-held. Democrats need to win 17 of the 35 for 51; Republicans need 19 of the 35 for 50. In the House, the Cook Political Report's October 23 ratings put 220 seats in the Solid, Likely or Lean Democrat columns, 198 in the Republican columns, and 17 as pure toss-ups. Sabato's Crystal Ball, October 20, rates 223 seats at least Leans Democrat, 200 at least Leans Republican, and 12 as toss-ups. These are ratings, not results.

How to read this page

Ratings, unofficial counts, certified results

Three different things, kept separate on purpose:

  • Ratings are projections. Every rating below names its forecaster (Cook Political Report, Sabato's Crystal Ball, or Inside Elections) and the date it was set. A rating describes competitiveness at one moment. It is not a result.
  • Election-night figures are unofficial. On November 3 this page will show votes and percent reporting exactly as each state's election authority publishes them, labelled unofficial, with the source and timestamp per race. States do not declare official winners on election night.
  • Certified results come later. States canvass and certify over the days and weeks after November 3. When a certified result exists, it replaces the unofficial figures on that race and is labelled Certified.

Two slow counts to plan for: Georgia's Senate race goes to a December 1 runoff if no candidate wins an outright majority. California accepts mail ballots postmarked by November 3 for up to 7 days after Election Day, so several California House races may stay uncalled for days. Maine and Alaska use ranked-choice voting, which also slows final tabulation. This page tracks key races only, so it does not show a live national House or Senate control total on election night: a control total is only honest with complete coverage, and complete live coverage would require all 50 state feeds.

Projection method for election night (tracker projections only, never overriding a certified result): where a state reports a percent reporting, estimated remaining votes = votes counted × (100 − percent reporting) / percent reporting. If the leader's margin is larger than the estimated remaining votes, the race is shown as Projected; if the margin is under 100 votes or under 0.5% of votes counted, it is Too close to call; otherwise the leader is shown as Leading. Where percent reporting is unknown, zero, or over 100, only Leading is shown. Candidates marked TBD are races where the general-election matchup could not be verified from a reputable source at build time; they are filled in when verified, never guessed.
