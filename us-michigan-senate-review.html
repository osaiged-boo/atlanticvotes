Michigan Senate review: El-Sayed wins the open seat

November 4, 2026

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed has won Michigan’s open seat in the United States Senate, defeating Republican Mike Rogers. The Associated Press called the race for El-Sayed.

Its latest figures put El-Sayed at 2,505,618 votes and Rogers at 2,170,524, with about 97 per cent of the expected vote counted. Reported shares put El-Sayed at about 52.5 per cent and Rogers at about 45.5 per cent.

El-Sayed is a physician and a former health director in Detroit and Wayne County. Rogers is a former congressman. The seat was open because two-term Democratic Senator Gary Peters retired. No Republican has won a Senate race in Michigan since 1994, and the race drew well over $100 million in advertising since the August primary.

When he takes his seat in January El-Sayed will become the first Muslim American elected to the United States Senate. That is the historic note of the race, and it is also why it drew attention well beyond Michigan.

The win keeps the seat in Democratic hands. Both of Michigan’s Senate seats were already held by Democrats, so the result does not meaningfully change the balance of the chamber.

One note on sourcing: Atlantic Votes did not carry Michigan live, because Michigan’s official feed could not be used for the tracker. This review is based on the Associated Press call and its latest figures, not on a live Atlantic Votes count.
