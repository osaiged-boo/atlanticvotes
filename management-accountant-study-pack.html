1 · The role, decoded

This is not a general management accountant job. Read the job description closely and a very specific picture emerges — and the panel will test you for exactly this.

What the job really is

A reporting, returns & forecasting-support role

The role sits in Financial Performance / FMI, supporting Finance Business Partners (FBPs) and senior managers — not owning one school's budget yourself. The core loop is:

  • Regulatory returns for the Office for Students (OfS): TRAC, Annual Financial Return (AFR), HEBCI
  • Monthly forecasting process — preparing data, GL postings and templates so FBPs forecast consistently
  • Reports for budget holders — maintaining and improving a suite of reports
  • Ad hoc analysis — pulling data from multiple systems, reconciling it, explaining it clearly
  • Training colleagues in data analysis and spreading best practice
What will decide the interview

The 5 things the panel is scoring

  • 1. Excel & data confidence (Criterion G) — intermediate/advanced, provable with examples
  • 2. Reconciliation mindset (F) — can you join messy data from different sources and prove it ties back?
  • 3. Forecasting & modelling (B + C) — budgets, forecasts, variance, assumptions
  • 4. Partnering style (D) — supportive, collegiate, clear with non-finance colleagues
  • 5. Process discipline (E) — clear process notes, audit trail, repeatability for a maternity-cover handover
Maternity-cover angle: Say it out loud — “I document as I go, so cover is seamless and the return to the post-holder is clean.” Panels hiring cover worry about continuity. Remove that worry in minute one.

3 · Behavioural questions — use STAR

LSBU uses its Behaviour Framework alongside the Career Pathways (this role: Organisational Services family). Even when a question sounds technical, weave in how you worked: collaborative, clear, inclusive, reliable.

The STAR formula — 2 minutes per answer

Situation (20 sec) → Task — your responsibility (20 sec) → Action — what you did, step by step (60 sec) → Result — numbers, deadline met, what you learned (20 sec). End with: “I’d bring that same approach here by…” to link back to LSBU.

Questions to ask them (pick 3 — always ask some)

  • “What does the monthly forecasting timetable look like, and where do FBPs find it hardest to forecast consistently?”
  • “Which systems feed TRAC and the AFR here, and how much of that process is already automated?”
  • “What handover is in place from the post-holder, and what would success look like in the first three months of the cover?”
  • “Which reports do budget holders rely on most — and which ones does the team most want to improve?”
  • “How is staff-time survey data for TRAC collected at LSBU, and what response rates do you typically get?”
  • “What training in data analysis would you most like this role to deliver to the wider team?”

Excel — the functions that will come up

Finance systems (ERP)

Unit4 (Agresso) / Oracle / SAP — the shared logic

  • General Ledger (GL): the master record of all transactions, coded by account + cost centre + project
  • Chart of accounts: the coding structure — get this right and reporting is easy; get it wrong and everything is a workaround
  • Journals: manual postings (accruals, corrections, recharges) — always with narrative, evidence and approver
  • Purchase to pay: requisition → PO → goods receipt → invoice → payment
  • Reporting: run reports by period/cost centre, export to Excel for analysis — never alter the source export; work on a copy and keep the raw file

Safe interview line: “Whichever ERP LSBU uses, my routine is the same: understand the coding structure first, reconcile my extract back to the GL control total, then analyse.”

Analysis & reporting tools

Power BI, Power Query & templates

  • Power Query (in Excel & Power BI): repeatable cleaning/merging — ideal for combining GL + HR + student data for TRAC
  • Power BI: interactive dashboards — measures (DAX e.g. SUM, CALCULATE), slicers by school/month, scheduled refresh
  • Forecasting templates: lock formula cells, colour inputs (one consistent colour = “type here”), include instructions tab, version + date in filename
  • Version control: 2026-27_Forecast_SchoolName_v3_2026-11-11.xlsx — never “final_FINAL.xlsx”

Golden rules of good financial modelling

  • Separate inputs, calculations and outputs — assumptions on one tab, clearly labelled, never hard-coded inside formulas
  • One formula per row/column, copied consistently — so errors are visible
  • Checks that must equal zero / agree to source — put them at the top, conditionally formatted red if they fail
  • No hard-coded numbers in formulas — link to the assumption cell so sensitivity is instant
  • Document: purpose, owner, date, sources, key assumptions — on the first tab

6 · The OfS returns — TRAC, AFR & HEBCI in depth

This is the most distinctive part of the job. Even basic, accurate fluency here will set you apart — most generic candidates won’t know these at all.

Return 1

TRAC

Transparent Approach to Costing. The sector-standard method UK providers use to calculate the full economic cost of teaching, research and other activities.

  • Allocates academic staff time (via time surveys / TAS) and support costs across activities
  • Adjusts financial statements to full economic cost (e.g. infrastructure adjustments)
  • Produces TRAC(T) for teaching — used in OfS funding and fee decisions
  • The role's part: staff-time surveying, checking data from other systems, supporting the calculations
Return 2

Annual Financial Return (AFR)

The OfS’s main annual collection of financial and student data from providers — the successor to the old HEFCE/HESA Finance Statistics Return in OfS terms.

  • Covers income, expenditure, balance sheet, cash flow, student numbers and forecasts
  • Must reconcile to the audited financial statements — any difference needs explaining
  • Used by OfS to monitor financial sustainability and condition of registration
Return 3

HEBCI

Higher Education Business & Community Interaction survey.

  • Captures income and activity from knowledge exchange: consultancy, contract research, CPD, facilities hire, spin-outs, community engagement
  • Feeds the Knowledge Exchange Framework (KEF) narrative
  • Data often sits outside finance — in research/enterprise teams — so chasing, definitions and evidence matter
Accuracy point for the interview: Don’t bluff submission dates or guidance versions — they change. Say: “I’d work to the current OfS/HESA guidance and timetable for that year, build a checklist from it, and reconcile every return back to the audited statements and GL before sign-off.” That answer is always correct.

How a good returns process runs — how to describe it

Plan from the guidance

Read the current OfS guidance, build a timetable with owners and deadlines, and flag the data you'll need from HR, student records, research & enterprise early.

Collect & survey

Run staff-time surveys for TRAC with clear comms and reminders, and chase HEBCI data from non-finance teams with definitions in plain English.

Reconcile everything

Tie totals to the GL / audited statements, compare to the prior year, and investigate movements beyond an agreed threshold.

Review, sign-off & document

Get a senior review, keep documented checks and an audit trail, submit on time — then write the process note while it’s fresh for next year (and for cover).

7 · Likely test scenarios — practise these out loud

Finance interviews at this level often include a short Excel task or a “talk me through this table” exercise. Rehearse the method, not just the answer.

Scenario A

“This school is £180k overspent. Walk us through it.”

The method: 1) Confirm the numbers tie to the GL. 2) Split the variance: volume vs price vs timing vs one-off. 3) Staff vs non-staff. 4) Ask: is it forecast to continue or reverse? 5) Identify what action is needed — and who owns it.

Say: “An overspend isn’t automatically bad — if student numbers and income are ahead too, the net position may be fine. I’d always show income and expenditure together.”

Scenario B

Excel task: messy GL download

The method: Keep the raw data untouched → make a clean copy (trim, consistent codes) → build a PivotTable by cost centre/account → run SUMIFS checks back to the raw total → highlight the top 5 variances → write a one-paragraph commentary: what, why, so what, next step.

If you get stuck on a function, narrate your logic — panels score method and checks, not just the final cell.

Scenario C

“Two systems disagree by £42k. What do you do?”

The method: Define what each system should contain → compare at transaction level → categorise the breaks (timing, missing, duplicate, coding) → quantify each → correct at source where possible → document everything. Never “plug” the difference.

Scenario D

“A budget holder says your report is wrong.”

The method: Stay collegiate → walk through it together line by line → often it’s commitments/accruals timing or a coding difference → if they’re right, fix it and thank them; if the report is right, explain with evidence → follow up in writing. This tests Criterion D directly.

8 · 7-day study plan & interview-day checklist

Your 7 days, about 1 hour a day

  • Day 1: Read Sections 1 + 6. Be able to explain TRAC, AFR and HEBCI in 30 seconds each.
  • Day 2: Work through Technical Q&A 1–8. Write your real numbers into two answers.
  • Day 3: Excel drills — build a PivotTable, XLOOKUP and SUMIFS from sample data. Practise Power Query Refresh.
  • Day 4: Work through Technical Q&A 9–end + the glossary. Test yourself: define 10 terms out loud.
  • Day 5: Behavioural — write your 4 STAR stories (partnering, deadline, error found, difficult colleague) and time them to 2 minutes.
  • Day 6: Practise Scenarios A–D out loud. Prepare your 3 questions to ask them.
  • Day 7: Do a mock interview start-to-finish. Print this pack. Get an early night.

Your first 30–60–90 days — if they ask

  • 30: Learn the systems, coding and timetables; read last year’s returns + process notes; meet the FBPs and budget holders; make no changes yet — listen first.
  • 60: Own the monthly forecast cycle end-to-end; deliver your first returns tasks; spot one report/template improvement and agree it with your manager.
  • 90: Be the confident owner of the process; documentation fully up to date for cover continuity; deliver a short data-analysis training session to the team.

Interview-day checklist

  • You can say “Why LSBU, why this role, why now” in 90 seconds — with the LSBU mission and Group model in it
  • You have 4 STAR stories ready, each with a number or clear outcome
  • You can define TRAC, AFR, HEBCI, accrual, variance and full economic cost without notes
  • You can name 5 Excel tools and give a real example of each
  • You know your qualification status precisely: papers passed, papers remaining, target completion date
  • You have 3 questions to ask the panel (see Section 3)
  • For a remote interview: you've tested the link, camera at eye level, water and a printed copy of this pack beside you — and a pen for the scenario task
