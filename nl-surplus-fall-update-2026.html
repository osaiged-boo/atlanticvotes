Newfoundland and Labrador swings from a $688M deficit to a $78M surplus

November 24, 2026

Finance Minister Craig Pardy released the 2026 Fall Fiscal and Economic Update on Monday, moving Newfoundland and Labrador from a budgeted deficit of $688 million in April to a projected surplus of $78 million for 2026-27, a $766-million swing driven by surging offshore oil revenue.

Revenue is now forecast at nearly $1.5 billion above the spring budget, with almost $1.1 billion of that in additional oil revenue. The budget assumed oil at $79 US a barrel; the update prices it at $98, with production forecast at 101.7 million barrels, up 16 per cent from last year. Oil royalties are now forecast at just over $3 billion, roughly a quarter of all provincial revenue.

The improved outlook also cuts the province’s net debt to $19.3 billion, down from the $20.8 billion budgeted in April. Even so, the government still borrows about $3.9 billion this fiscal year, which ends in March 2027, and pays about $1.2 billion in debt servicing. The economy is expected to grow 7.1 per cent this year, the strongest in Canada.

Pardy warned the windfall should not be mistaken for a fix. “Let me be clear: that is one good year. It does not mean that the problem is fixed,” he told reporters in St. John’s. The government says 80 per cent of the windfall will go to debt repayment and 20 per cent to affordability measures, including tax cuts and cheques for seniors and families. Spending is also up $704 million above the spring budget, with about $400 million of that in health care.

Opposition critics were unimpressed. Liberal Leader John Hogan said the government is taking credit for oil prices it does not control and wants long-term cost-of-living measures instead. NDP critic Sheilagh O’Leary said the province needs a real plan for when oil prices fall.
