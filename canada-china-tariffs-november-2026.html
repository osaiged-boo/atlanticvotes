Canada and China widen tariff relief in Shenzhen deal

November 18, 2026

Prime Minister Mark Carney and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed in Shenzhen on Tuesday to widen tariff relief between their two countries, with China cutting tariffs on about $400 million in Canadian exports and Canada extending tariff remissions on some Chinese steel and aluminium.

The relief covers $116 million in frozen pork cuts and $285 million in fish and seafood, including frozen cold-water shrimp and prawns, Pacific spot prawn, molluscs including geoduck, sea cucumber and Greenland halibut. The deal extends and expands the preliminary trade arrangement the two countries reached in January, and builds on a two-year extension of relief for canola meal, peas, lobster and crab announced earlier this month.

In return, Canada will extend by two years its remission measures for certain Chinese steel and aluminium products now set to expire at the end of 2026, and add 32 more tariff items where Canadian supply is insufficient or unavailable. Ottawa says it has now restored market access for more than 90 per cent of the trade hit by Chinese tariffs, worth nearly $7.5 billion in Canadian exports.

The leaders also announced a Canadian trade mission to China in 2027, $850,000 in scholarships for 850 Canadian students to study in China, and new Air Canada flights from Vancouver to Guangzhou starting in May 2027. Carney said Canada was well ahead of schedule in its goal of boosting exports to China by 50 per cent by the end of the decade, hailing “greater predictability and stability” in the relationship. It was the third meeting between Carney and Xi since Carney became prime minister.

Pork producers welcomed the deal, with Canadian Meat Council chief executive Kyle Larkin calling it “a great step in the right direction”, while the Canadian Steel Producers Association said the steel items covered are niche and should have little effect on domestic mills. In Washington, White House trade adviser Peter Navarro asked on X whether Carney was “really threatening to play China off the U.S.”
