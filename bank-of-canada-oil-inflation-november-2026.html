Bank of Canada expects second round of oil-linked inflation in 2027, Macklem says

November 24, 2026

Bank of Canada governor Tiff Macklem says high energy prices will keep stinging consumers at the fuel pump and beyond in 2027, even as oil prices fall from recent peaks. Speaking Tuesday at an event hosted by the Economic Club of Canada in Toronto, he warned a second round of the recent oil shock linked to the war in the Middle East will stoke inflation next year, dampening hopes that the recent drop in benchmark oil prices would bring meaningful relief.

“This second-round effect of the oil shock will add to inflation persistence,” Macklem said. So far, he added, businesses have largely absorbed the higher production and transportation costs linked to high energy prices, but historical lags and the Bank’s own business surveys point to more of those costs reaching consumers in the year ahead.

October’s inflation data gave little evidence yet of high oil prices spilling into other parts of the economy. The consumer price index rose 3.1 per cent on a year-over-year basis last month, matching September’s 3.1 per cent increase.

Last month the Bank of Canada raised its policy interest rate by 25 basis points to 2.5 per cent, ending a year-long pause at 2.25 per cent with its first increase since 2023. In their summary of deliberations, published November 10, policymakers said further hikes may be needed as high oil prices and rising business costs collide with a resilient economy, risking broad-based inflation through 2027.

In its October Monetary Policy Report, the Bank called for inflation to remain near three per cent through much of 2027, easing toward two per cent, the midpoint of its target range, in 2028. Since then, global oil benchmarks have fallen to roughly three-month lows, helped in part by potential military de-escalation between the United States and Iran and by rising exports of Middle Eastern oil through the Strait of Hormuz, the vital shipping route heavily disrupted by the conflict.

Looking ahead, Macklem said the Bank is preparing to respond to “larger and more frequent supply shocks,” warning inflation is likely to be pushed off course more frequently and for longer. Monetary policy cannot lower global energy prices, he said, but it can limit how much they spill over, with the goal of avoiding a situation where Canadians face rising prices for a wide range of goods and services.

The Bank makes its final rate decision of 2026 on December 9. Ahead of that meeting, its governing council will have two fresh data points to consider: Statistics Canada releases third-quarter gross domestic product on November 30, followed by the November Labour Force Survey on December 4.

Tuesday’s speech was one of Macklem’s first major public appearances since announcing earlier this month that he will not seek a second term, and will step down when his seven-year mandate expires on June 2, 2027.
